imagine a 20 year old man in a very remote village of UP, India.

he's doing BSc from a nearby college, helps his father in his farming work and learns programming in whatever free time he gets.

2 years of his graduation are completed and he has only one year left to prepare himself for a job so that he can move out of his village and doesn't have to choose farming as his career.

in the last two years, he has learnt only html, css and javascript from freecodecamp, built some scrappy projects, doesn't have any internship experience, doesn't know anyone who's related to tech, and doesn't even know what to learn next.

every day, he lives in panic. not knowing whether he'll even get a job or he'll have to accept his fate and quit.

just to make things worse, he's also facing financial problems, health problems (severe allergies), and family problems.

that 20 year old man was me in early 2023.

and [this](/blog/my-work-so-far) was me by the end of 2025.

when i look back, it feels like magic to me.

but i know it was not the magic but **Open Source**.

i already shared how [open source changed my life](/blog/my-journey-of-open-source), so in this article im gonna share why you should do open source?

no BS. no motivational porn. just some logical reasons ive realized so far.

## 1. solid skills with real proof of work.

Open Source projects are actual products — either commercial companies or non-profits.

millions of people use them. so when you contribute to them, you build real skills by working on a real product.

that's what differentiates you from the ones who build AI-slop projects, penetrate "Agent" into it and sound like Elon.

and that's exactly why contributing to Open Source is hard. (initially)

## 2. POW that markets itself. always.

your open source contributions are always public. anyone from anywhere in the world can see them anytime.

you contributed to a project and left after some time but your contributions are still marketing you.

and they'll always do.

![Andreas Wennberg reaching out on LinkedIn](/assets/blog/recognized-me.png)

## 3. a place where only the "real work" matters.

in open source, no one cares about why you're wearing boxers while working. (i wear boxers.)

no one cares at what time you wake up and start working.

no one cares about the certificates you have, the college you went to, or the country you come from.

here only your "work" matters. that's your only identity.

## 4. connectionmaxxing

as i mentioned above, when you contribute to real products, you also talk to actual CTOs, CEOs and tech leads when you contribute to Open Source.

and that's how you truly build real connections that are worth more than gold.

not by spamming "let's connect" on linkedin.

i contributed to this company and their official account followed me on twitter.

![Polar following me on Twitter](/assets/blog/polar-following-me.png)

## 5. asymmetric results

open source has asymmetric results.

you contribute to an open source project thinking you will get an opportunity but in the long run you end up getting a lot more than an opportunity.

you end up around really cool and talented people.

you end up working with people globally.

you end up getting a visa for a new country.

you end up being in places you've never dreamt of.

Guillermo Rauch's open source contributions helped him get an O-1 Visa to work in the USA.

![Guillermo Rauch on open source contributions and O-1 visa](/assets/blog/guillermo-rauch-o1-visa.png)

## 6. money

elephant in the room. money.

good news, open source doesn't disappoint you here as well.

in open source there're countless ways to make money.

some of them are:

1. internship/job/contract
2. bounties
3. creating own oss project
4. charging enterprises for consultation
5. by teaching and content
6. by maintaining a community
7. donations.
8. etc.

here you do the work that helps people and money comes to you.

## 7. freedom

needless to say, open source is probably the best thing when it comes to the freedom of location, freedom of work, freedom of people and freedom of time.

you can be traveling to Mount Fuji and working on your open source project without taking a leave.

## 8. future

more and more companies are going open source.

for transparency and trust.

also it's incredibly easy to find the best talent when your codebase is open source.

here, a founder doesn't have to interview thousands of applicants to find a good engineer.

they already have someone contributing to their codebase for a long time. and he/she is a sure shot.

along with this, more VCs are funding Open-Source-first companies.

![Open source market projected to reach $182 billion by 2034](/assets/blog/oss-future.png)

this indicates a bright future of open source.

OSS/ACC for the WIN!!
