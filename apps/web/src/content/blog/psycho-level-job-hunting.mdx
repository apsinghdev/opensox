the economy of blindly spamming applications is already DEAD.

if your goal is to work at great startups, DO THIS instead:

## 1. create a doc (notion/obsidian)

and add all the startups you wanna work for in this doc.

just add company site, founders' twitter and emails (if you can get them)

## 2. grow this list

after that, whenever you explore and find a good company that is suitable for you and you wanna work for it, just add that company to your list.

## 3. rawdogg the company

now do these things.

- follow the founders on twitter (must)
- follow the team members
- TIP: check the company's official twitter acc and go to its follower list. which company's founders and team don't follow their own company's twitter? you got it.
- if founders and team aren't on twitter, remove that company from the list.
- know more about the company
- if their codebase is open source, tell your mom you hit the jackpot
- start contributing and interacting with them

for the non-tech roles:

- observe problems they're facing while building the community, marketing, etc
- brainstorm ideas to solve those
- create a detailed doc
- pitch them (by cold dm/email)

## 4. interact

interact with the founders and their team members.

now you're equipped with the knowledge and you know a lot about their company.

you should be able to hold a good conversation with them.

so interact in their comments, suggest ideas, ask creative questions and most importantly, do all of these without giving jerk vibes.

## 5. iterate

while you're doing these things, start checking if the companies in your list are hiring or not.

if they do, you already have your homework done.

just ping the founder/CTO/someone who's working in that company and knows you very well.

and finally, show a middle finger to the job market competition.

## tips

- definitely follow the founders and team members who have fewer followers. they're more likely to reply to you.
- use these resources to find breakout startups hiring before everyone else:
  - Ramp's monthly vendor reports
  - Harmonic's quarterly Hot 25
  - a16z Build newsletter
  - Founders You Should Know
  - Next Play newsletter
  - YC startup directory
  - Early Days Substack

BOTTOM LINE: quality of application always beats quantity of application.

my personal philosophy is simple:

- pick a few companies you love to work for
- just obsess with those
- build connections with the people working there
- spot the right time (as a byproduct of the 3rd point)
