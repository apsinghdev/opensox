<p class="blog-intro">these are the questions asked by the pro members in the Opensox Pro community and my answers to them</p>

> could you guide me on how i should grind for the next 3 months so i can maximize my chances of landing a good-paying role, ideally around 1 lpm?

for this decide three things first:

1. type of companies you wanna work for
2. pay you want (you already did that)
3. role

once you know the company and role, you'll know what you should learn.

for the applied ai role, i'd suggest you learn it fundamentally. you don't have to spend research role level time like months, but understanding fundamental things like ml algos, transformers, math concepts will differentiate you from avg engineers.

speaking of tech stack, be an engineer first. tech stack will keep on changing. learn the fundamentals and first principles and the rest you can learn anything on the go. to be an engineer read this: [how to be an engineer](/blog/how-to-be-an-engineer)

> should i pick gsoc orgs based on what i'm currently learning, especially in the rag/ai space? i've already had 2–3 interview reachouts, and most of them are looking for an ai + full stack engineer.

as we're quite early so you don't have to think very much yet. you can simply pick an org where you're feeling good about the maintainers and can make ur first contribution and then second and so on.

don't worry about things you don't know right now. just master two things — fundamentals and first principle and you can learn everything when it is required. every engineer does things like that.

> i'm hesitating a little about jumping to a new language, so i'd really appreciate your take on whether learning python is actually necessary for the direction i'm trying to take !!!!

you shouldn't overthink much. learning python will take hardly 1-2 hrs along w a small project. fundamentally every language contains the same things — loops, functions, classes, etc, so if you know these in one, learning in another won't be a big problem
